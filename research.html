Research

Develop the three complementary analytical views, then use neural-network feedback to test and revise assumptions. Established foundations and open questions are distinguished below.

From a given representation
to its formation.

Fixed, local and global analyses address different questions. Together they help us understand the statistical quality of features and how that quality changes during training.

Characterize learning with a given representation.

Hold a feature map fixed and analyze how the target, spectrum, noise and estimator jointly determine prediction risk. Fine-grained risk, ESD and FEM provide tools for evaluating representations.

Understand how beneficial features form and persist.

Follow the joint trajectory of features and coefficients. Study when initialization, parameterization and training mechanisms favor feature formation, and whether those features persist through later training.

Let network behavior
inform the theory.

A concrete correspondence makes three observations useful: how representations change, what they predict after refitting, and how the original network generalizes. These observations guide model refinement and test theoretical conditions.

01

Observe the representation

Feature spectra & empirical alignment

Track feature spectra and empirical target-alignment measures during training; compare the observed changes with the theoretical picture.

02

Evaluate predictive capacity

Freeze features. Refit independently.

Freeze a learned representation and fit a predictor on independent samples to evaluate the predictions those features can support.

03

Compare the full learning process

Generalization of the original network

Examine performance on new data alongside representation measures and refitting results to connect feature learning with the original training process.

Initial experiments with fully connected, convolutional and residual networks provide observations for this process. The three evaluations address different objects and should be interpreted together.

Use established tools
to explore the next questions.

Results in specified models offer starting points for a more general analysis of dynamic representations, nonlinear coupling and the conditions for beneficial feature formation.

Existing foundations

  1. Fine-grained risk analysis

    Learning curves, optimality, high-dimensional rates, saturation and risk equivalence for fixed representations.

    Read paper ↗

  2. Representation quality

    Effective Span Dimension (ESD), Feature Error Measure (FEM), and the role of target alignment.

    Read paper ↗

  3. Tractable adaptive models

    Over-parameterized sequence models, diagonal adaptive kernels and specific directional models.

    Read paper ↗

Questions to advance

  1. Risk for dynamic representations

    Handle spectral perturbations and the dependence of trained representations on the sample.

  2. General nonlinear coupling

    Understand when local approximations apply and how they may translate into generalization gains.

  3. Formation and retention of useful features

    Analyze the conditions induced by common initializations and concrete training mechanisms.
